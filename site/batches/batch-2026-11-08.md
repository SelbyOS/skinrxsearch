# [ENTRY] Topical 3% tranexamic acid and fine lines: an exploratory wrinkle result from a melasma trial, plus a cell-culture mechanism
- **Verdict:** WATCH | **Lane:** Goals-aligned science | **Run:** 2026-11-08
- **Evidence:** 2 of 5. Exploratory secondary analysis of an RCT (open label as far as stated) plus in-vitro work; full text read
- **Human trial:** yes | **Skin-tone data:** Fitzpatrick III to V (III 18%, IV 78%, V 4%); Chinese women, 18 to 60
- **Tags:** tranexamic acid, melasma, periorbital lines, GPR30, fibroblast senescence, exploratory analysis, China, Fitzpatrick III-V, RCT secondary, cosmetic

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
In 50 women with melasma, a 3% tranexamic acid serum was followed by a small drop in periorbital wrinkle score versus moisturizer alone; the wrinkle result was not the trial's planned outcome, and the mechanism comes from fibroblasts in a dish.
- Observation (full text read): 50 women with melasma, phototypes III to V (about 78% IV), were randomised 1:1 to 3% tranexamic acid serum plus moisturizer or moisturizer alone, twice daily for 8 weeks, followed to week 12. All 50 completed.
- Observation: periorbital wrinkle score (modified Fitzpatrick scale, graded blind by two dermatologists) changed by -0.43 vs +0.03 at week 12 (p<0.001). Baseline scores were low (about 1.4 to 1.6), so this is early fine lines, not deep wrinkles.
- Observation: the wrinkle analysis was post hoc. The authors say they noticed it during a melasma trial. The melasma trial itself is a separate paper that I did not open.
- Inference: the cell work (D-galactose-aged fibroblasts at 200 ug/mL, GPR30 blocking and knockdown, docking) is mechanistic support at best; D-galactose senescence is a lab model and 200 ug/mL is not a skin concentration.
- Limits: single hospital, no vehicle-matched serum (control was moisturizer only), no blinding statement for participants, no collagen measurement.

## FIT AND REPLACEMENT LOGIC (my judgment, not a measurement)
- **Fit for you:** Partial Pigment-prone skin: Partial. Tranexamic acid is already a pigment-lane ingredient; the new part is a possible fine-line side benefit that has not been confirmed.
- **Barrier:** Unknown. Barrier function and irritation were not reported in the text I read.
- **Retinoid:** Unknown. Other anti-wrinkle products and procedures were prohibited during the trial, so use with a retinoid is untested.
- **Breakout risk:** Unknown. Not assessed.
- **Season:** Partial. Pigment-lane ingredient; daily SPF is standard alongside it, though not stated as a protocol element here.
- **What it displaces:** Nothing new. If a tranexamic acid serum is already in the pigment step, this adds a reason to keep it, not a reason to add a step.

## WHAT IT IS
Tranexamic acid is a lysine-analogue originally used to reduce bleeding. Topical and oral forms are used for melasma. This paper reports an unplanned look at periorbital fine lines in the trial arm that used a 3% serum, and adds fibroblast experiments.

## MECHANISM
3% tranexamic acid serum, twice daily [applied] -> GPR30 up, MAPK phosphorylation down [cell] -> Lower senescence and matrix-degrading signals [cell] -> Lower periorbital wrinkle score [human]
1. Applied: 3% tranexamic acid serum twice daily on the face for 8 weeks (human trial).
2. Stated by the authors: tranexamic acid raised GPR30 levels and lowered MAPK phosphorylation in D-galactose-stressed fibroblasts, with fewer senescence markers (SA-beta-gal, p16, p21) and lower IL-6, IL-8, MMP1 and MMP3 expression.
3. Cell evidence: blocking GPR30 with G15 or knocking it down weakened these effects, which supports GPR30 involvement in that model.
4. Inferred, not shown: that the same pathway operates in living human dermis at topical doses and explains the wrinkle score change. No collagen, elastin or skin biopsy data were collected.
- **Inference, not observation:** The step from fibroblast culture to the wrinkle score is the authors' hypothesis. The human result and the cell result were not linked by any measurement in the same people.

## STUDIES
### Lin Y, Wang Y, Wang W, et al. Tranexamic acid protects human dermal fibroblasts from D-galactose-induced senescence via the GPR30/MAPK pathway. Ann Med 2026;58(1):2663263.
- **Read depth:** Full text (open access, PMC) read
- **Design:** Exploratory analysis of a randomised, moisturizer-controlled trial (Xiangya Hospital, China, June to December 2021), 1:1, 25 per arm; plus fibroblast experiments (primary cells from circumcised foreskin of donors aged 5 to 20).
- **Who was included:** Women 18 to 60 with facial melasma, phototypes II to IV by protocol (III to V enrolled); excluded thromboembolic disease, anticoagulants, pregnancy, and use of other depigmenting or anti-wrinkle products. Mean age 36.7.
- **Effect size:** About 0.4 scale points on a scale whose baseline here was about 1.5; the paper does not give a minimal clinically important difference.
- **Placebo comparison:** Control was moisturizer alone, so the serum vehicle was not matched. The control group returned to baseline by week 12, which is what a stable no-effect arm looks like.
- **Material outcome:** Unclear. A 0.4-point change on a low-baseline grading scale is visible to blinded graders but I cannot say it is noticeable to the wearer; no patient-rated outcome is reported.
- **Adverse events:** Not reported in the text I read.
- **Funding and conflicts:** No funding or conflict statement located in the text I read; check the front matter.
- **Limits:** Post hoc analysis of a trial built for melasma; unblinded participants likely (serum vs none); one centre; no biopsy or collagen data; fibroblasts from young male donors and an artificial ageing model.
  - Week 4: change -0.17 vs -0.05 (p=0.007). Week 8: -0.44 vs -0.02 (p<0.001). Week 12: -0.43 vs +0.03 (p<0.001).
  - Baseline-adjusted means at week 12: 1.08 vs 1.60 (p<0.001).
  - Inter-rater agreement between the two graders was high (ICC 0.89 to 0.92).
  - Cell results: lower ROS and MDA, higher SOD and catalase activity with tranexamic acid; effects reduced by GPR30 blocking or knockdown.

- It works through GPR30 | source: Cell experiments with an antagonist and knockdown | Supported in the dish only; docking energy of -5.4 kcal/mol is a computational suggestion, not binding data.

The pigment pathways are not tone-specific, so a similar effect is plausible by mechanism, but that is inference. Fine-line grading scales are also less validated on deeper skin.
