# [ENTRY] Thiamidol (isobutylamido thiazolyl resorcinol)
- **Verdict:** WATCH | **Lane:** Ingredient | **Run:** 2026-10-25-test
- **Evidence:** 3 of 5. Randomized vehicle-controlled trial (200 people, 12 weeks) + uncontrolled real-world study + small pilots; brand-affiliated authors on most
- **Human trial:** yes | **Skin-tone data:** partial (phototypes I-V in one study; none reported in the RCT abstract)
- **Tags:** pigment, tyrosinase inhibitor, melasma, PIH, non-hydroquinone, cosmetic/OTC, brand-affiliated evidence, needs head-to-head, ship from EU

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
A human-tyrosinase inhibitor with one sizable vehicle-controlled trial (about 36% vs 16% mMASI drop at 12 weeks), but the evidence is brand-linked and the trials I could read did not report deep skin tones.
- Mechanism: Thiamidol inhibits human tyrosinase, the rate-limiting enzyme of melanin synthesis. It is not an exfoliant or irritant class, which matters for pigment-prone skin (my inference).
- Best trial: 200 people with facial hyperpigmentation, 12 weeks, 0.2% cream vs vehicle. mMASI fell 36.1% vs 16.1%. Patient global assessment and digital colour analysis did not differ between groups, so the visible benefit is less certain than the physician score.
- Skin tone: the real-world study covered phototypes I-V only. The randomized trial's abstract does not report phototype.

## FIT AND REPLACEMENT LOGIC (my judgment, not a measurement)
- **Fit for you:** Partial Mechanistically plausible for pigment-prone deep skin because it targets the enzyme directly rather than irritating the skin. Untested head to head against hydroquinone, azelaic acid or tranexamic acid, and untested in phototype VI.
- **Barrier:** Partial. Not an exfoliant or acid class, so barrier burden should be low (my inference). Tolerability was reported as good in the trials, but tolerance in deep skin was not separately reported.
- **Retinoid:** Unknown. No data on use alongside retinoids. Introduce alone first.
- **Breakout risk:** Unknown. Vehicle-dependent. The products are cosmetic creams and serums; I did not read an ingredient list.
- **Season:** Partial. UV and visible-light exposure drive pigment. One lab study suggests Thiamidol blunts visible-light darkening, but it was in healthy skin and brand-run.
- **What it displaces:** Only an existing non-hydroquinone brightening serum used in the off-cycle slot. If that slot is already filled, it fits nowhere: adding it as a further pigment step is the usual failure.

## WHAT IT IS
Thiamidol (INCI: Isobutylamido Thiazolyl Resorcinol) is a synthetic resorcinol derivative developed by Beiersdorf and identified through screening against human tyrosinase rather than the mushroom enzyme older actives were tested on. It is the featured active in Eucerin's Anti-Pigment range. It is a cosmetic ingredient in the markets I looked at; I did not verify its regulatory status in every country.
- **Structure:** C13H14N2O3S, SMILES CC(C)C(=O)Nc1nc(cs1)-c1ccc(O)cc1O (flat 2D, stereochemistry omitted). Record: https://cymitquimica.com/cas/1428450-95-6/

## MECHANISM
Applied as 0.2% cream (RCT) [applied] -> Inhibits human tyrosinase [stated] -> Less melanin synthesis [inferred] -> Blunts UVB- and visible-light-induced darkening [human] -> mMASI fell 36% vs 16% on vehicle [human]
1. Thiamidol binds human tyrosinase and inhibits it (stated in the papers I read; the binding data are not in the abstracts I read).
2. Less tyrosinase activity means less conversion of tyrosine to melanin precursors.
3. Less melanin synthesis means less new pigment in spots or after UV or visible-light exposure.
- **Inference, not observation:** The abstracts I read report outcomes (colour, mMASI), not the enzyme chemistry. Mechanism statements come from the authors' framing and a review abstract. Whether the effect holds in epidermis with high melanocyte activity is inference.

## STUDIES
### Lekhavat C, Rojanamatin J, Suphannaphong M, Kolbe L. Efficacy and Tolerability of 0.2% Thiamidol Cream for Facial Hyperpigmentation: A Randomized, Double-Blind, and Vehicle-Controlled Study. Dermatol Ther (Heidelb). 2026;16(3):1551-1565.
- **Read depth:** Abstract only (a full-text link exists; not opened)
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled, 12 weeks. 200 randomized, 196 completed. Registered NCT03926845.
- **Who was included:** People of both sexes with facial hyperpigmentation. The abstract does not state phototype or ethnicity; study centre is in Thailand.
- **Effect size:** Roughly 20 percentage points more mMASI reduction than vehicle at 12 weeks (arithmetic from the reported percentages).
- **Placebo comparison:** The vehicle group improved by 16% on mMASI, so a large share of the Thiamidol arm's change is not specific to the active.
- **Material outcome:** Not established. The physician score moved but the patient-rated and instrument measures did not separate the groups.
- **Adverse events:** No significant adverse events reported in either group (abstract).
- **Funding and conflicts:** A Beiersdorf author is on the paper; a Thai government pharmaceutical organization is also an affiliation. Funding statement not read.
- **Limits:** Abstract read only. Mixed hyperpigmentation types; the abstract gives no breakdown by melasma. No phototype data in the abstract; no post-treatment follow-up reported.
  - mMASI reduction from baseline, Thiamidol vs vehicle: 11.8% vs 5.4% (week 4), 27.9% vs 13.6% (week 8), 36.1% vs 16.1% (week 12), all P<0.001.
  - Physician global assessment: slight to moderate improvement at weeks 8 and 12.
  - Patient global assessment and digital colour analysis: no significant difference between groups.
  - Freckles and solar lentigines: no significant difference.
### Schuster B, Rogers T, Vendruscolo C, et al. Real-world evidence for the efficacy and tolerability of an isobutylamido-thiazolyl-resorcinol (Thiamidol)-containing skincare regimen on facial hyperpigmentation and overall skin appearance: A multi-centre study in 11 European countries. Int J Cosmet Sci. 2026.
- **Read depth:** Abstract only
- **Design:** Multi-centre, uncontrolled, open-label, 12 weeks, 11 European countries; regimen containing Thiamidol (not Thiamidol alone).
- **Who was included:** 629 completers, 97.8% women, mean age 47, phototypes I-V. Phototype VI not included.
- **Effect size:** Large percentages from an uncontrolled study with a multi-product regimen. They cannot be attributed to Thiamidol alone.
- **Placebo comparison:** None. Uncontrolled.
- **Material outcome:** Not established; the percentages are relative and uncontrolled.
- **Adverse events:** Not detailed in the abstract beyond the tolerability ratings.
- **Funding and conflicts:** Most authors are Beiersdorf employees.
- **Limits:** No control arm, regimen rather than single ingredient, brand-run, no phototype VI. Treat as a safety and tolerability signal, not an efficacy estimate.
  - Reported relative improvements at 12 weeks: skin evenness 91%, radiance 82%, smoothness 73%; mMASI -76%.
  - Dermatologists rated tolerability very good in 81.1% of participants.
### Mann T, Eggers K, Rogers T, et al. Visible light induces skin darkening in vivo: comparative pilot studies reveal enhanced susceptibility in melasma and its mitigation by a human tyrosinase inhibitor. Photochem Photobiol Sci. 2026;25(2):299-306.
- **Read depth:** Abstract only
- **Design:** Pilot in vivo studies: visible light and UV exposure on melasma and perilesional skin; Thiamidol versus vehicle on repeated visible-light irradiation in healthy skin.
- **Who was included:** Melasma patients and healthy volunteers; sample sizes and phototypes are not in the abstract.
- **Effect size:** Not in the abstract.
- **Placebo comparison:** Vehicle control in the healthy-skin part.
- **Material outcome:** Not established; an induced-darkening model, not treatment of existing spots.
- **Adverse events:** Not reported in the abstract.
- **Funding and conflicts:** All authors are from Beiersdorf R&D.
- **Limits:** Pilot, brand-run, abstract only. Visible light matters for deep skin tones, but this does not show the product replaces tinted, iron-oxide sun protection.
  - Melasma lesions darkened more than surrounding skin after visible light.
  - In healthy skin, Thiamidol significantly reduced visible-light-induced darkening compared with vehicle.
### Vachiramon V, Kositkuljorn C, Leerunyakul K, Chanprapaph K. Isobutylamido thiazolyl resorcinol for prevention of UVB-induced hyperpigmentation. J Cosmet Dermatol. 2020;20(3):987-992.
- **Read depth:** Abstract only
- **Design:** Randomized, single-blinded, split-arm pilot; 3 weeks of application then UVB-induced spots.
- **Who was included:** 30 healthy participants, Bangkok, Thailand. Phototype not stated in the abstract.
- **Effect size:** Not extracted from the abstract.
- **Placebo comparison:** Control arm without the product (split-arm).
- **Material outcome:** Not established.
- **Adverse events:** No significant side effects (abstract).
- **Funding and conflicts:** Not checked.
- **Limits:** 30 healthy people, UVB-induced spots only, abstract only. Single-blinded.
  - After UVB induction, the Thiamidol-treated arm had a significantly lower lightness index than control and returned to normal colour earlier.
  - No significant side effects.

## HOW TO ADAPT IT
- Treat it as a swap into the brightening-serum slot, not an addition. If a non-hydroquinone brightener is already in use, trial this in its place for the full 12 weeks the trial used.
- Use as directed on the product; the only studied strength I saw is 0.2%.
- Sun protection is part of any pigment routine: visible light is implicated (see the Mann abstract), so tinted, iron-oxide protection is the larger lever.
- Introduce it alone for one to two weeks before pairing it with an acid or retinoid, because combination data are absent.

## WHAT TO EXPECT, AND WHEN
- **Weeks 1-4:** Little visible change. In the RCT the mMASI difference was modest at week 4.
- **Weeks 8-12:** The range in which the trial separated from vehicle. Expect gradual change, and note the vehicle group also improved.
- **After stopping:** No follow-up data in the abstracts I read.

## SAFETY
- **Contraindications and unknowns:** No adverse events reported in the abstracts. Not tested in pregnancy or in combination with prescription depigmenting agents. Treat the absence of reports as unknown.
- **Side benefits:** None shown. The visible-light result is a protective effect on induced darkening in healthy skin, not a treatment effect.

## DOES IT TRANSFER?
Does it transfer to deeper skin? The mechanism (inhibiting the enzyme that makes melanin) is not tone-specific, so I would expect some effect. But the real-world study stops at phototype V, the RCT abstract reports no phototype, and the responses of high-melanin skin to a tyrosinase inhibitor alone are not shown here. That is inference, not evidence.

- A head-to-head or add-on trial against hydroquinone, tranexamic acid or azelaic acid

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
A fresh review argues the two scalp conditions overlap (SD in 40-50% of AGA patients) and should be managed by restoring scalp barrier and microbiome balance together; it is a framework, not a product or a trial.
- Review abstract (read): SD is present in 40-50% of AGA patients; shared factors proposed include androgen-driven sebum, microbial imbalance, barrier dysfunction and inflammatory signalling. The authors call for combined, individualized management.

# [ENTRY] Mevalonolactone (MVL / mevalonic acid)
- **Verdict:** WATCH | **Lane:** Ingredient | **Run:** 2026-10-12
- **Evidence:** 2 of 5. Small human RCT (2 weeks) + cell and 3D-epidermis work; ingredient is a commercial supplier product
- **Human trial:** yes | **Skin-tone data:** not reported
- **Tags:** barrier, TEWL, ceramide-adjacent lipids, PPAR, ABCA12, fermentation-derived, OTC/cosmetic, winter-dry skin, needs second trial

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
A barrier-lipid signal backed by one small, short, double-blind human trial. The louder collagen and hyaluronic-acid claims trace to brand statements, not papers I could find.
- Mechanism proposed by the authors: MVL becomes mevalonic acid in water, raises PPAR beta/delta, which raises the lipid transporter ABCA12, which moves free fatty acids into lamellar granules that build the skin's barrier lipids.
- In the trial, the placebo group's water loss got worse over 2 winter weeks and the MVL group's did not. The win is protection against deterioration, not improvement from baseline.
- Nothing visible should be expected at 2 weeks. The endpoints were instrument readings.

## FIT AND REPLACEMENT LOGIC (my judgment, not a measurement)
- **Fit for you:** Partial Plausible for dry-season barrier support, including on retinoid-treated skin (my inference). Weak fit as an acne or sebum fix: the trial population had dry skin and measured water loss, not breakouts.
- **Barrier:** Partial. Two-week, double-blind trial in dry-skinned women showed protection against water-loss worsening. No data in deeper skin tones.
- **Retinoid:** Unknown. Not tested on retinoid-treated skin. Fit with a retinoid routine is my inference only.
- **Breakout risk:** Unknown. No comedogenicity or acne data found. Risk depends mostly on the vehicle's richness, which no product discloses.
- **Season:** Strong. The trial ran January to February, and the benefit was preventing winter worsening. Fits the dry months.
- **What it displaces:** A basic barrier moisturizer. It should replace that step, not add a step.

## WHAT IT IS
Mevalonolactone (INCI: Mevalonolactone) is the lactone form of mevalonic acid, a molecule early in the body's cholesterol and isoprenoid pathway. The cosmetic raw material in the trial is produced by fermentation with a sake-related yeast (Saccharomycopsis fibuligera) and sold by ADEKA (Japan) under the name Luminacare. Supplier materials position it as a moisturizing and anti-aging agent.
- **Structure:** C6H10O3, SMILES CC1(O)CCOC(=O)C1 (flat 2D, stereochemistry omitted). Record: https://foodb.ca/compounds/FDB023804

## MECHANISM
Applied at 0.1% in a lotion [applied] -> Reverts to mevalonic acid in water [stated] -> PPAR beta/delta signal up 1.7 times [cell] -> ABCA12 lipid transporter up [cell] -> More lamellar granules and free fatty acids [cell] -> Stronger barrier lipid layer [inferred] -> Less water loss over 2 winter weeks [human]
1. Applied topically, MVL reverts to mevalonic acid on contact with water (stated in the paper).
2. In cultured keratinocytes, PPAR beta/delta mRNA rose 1.7-fold and ABCA12 mRNA and protein rose.
3. ABCA12 is the transporter that loads lipids into lamellar granules, the packets that later become the lipid mortar between skin cells.
4. Cells showed more and larger lamellar granules, and free fatty acids were higher (myristic acid about 4.6x, palmitic about 3.6x, palmitoleic about 3.3x).
5. Result claimed: less water loss through the stratum corneum.
- **Inference, not observation:** The chain from PPAR to ABCA12 to granules is the authors' inference from cell data. They also argue the effect is unlikely to run through cholesterol, because fatty-acid synthesis enzymes did not change. None of this was shown directly in human skin.

## STUDIES
### Chen B, Lu N, Lee K, Ye L, Hasegawa C, Maeda K, et al. Application of mevalonolactone prevents deterioration of epidermal barrier function by accelerating the lamellar granule lipid transport system. Skin Res Technol. 2022;28(6):804-814.
- **Read depth:** Full text (open access) read
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group. 66 women aged 30-60 enrolled; 30 active and 29 placebo analyzed. Two weeks, January-February 2020. Lotion with 0.1% MVL versus the same lotion without it, applied to the face twice daily.
- **Who was included:** Healthy women with low stratum corneum water, high water loss and low surface lipid. Skin disease (atopic dermatitis, psoriasis, ichthyosis) excluded. Mean age about 45. Skin tone and ethnicity are not reported in the text I read. The study site is not stated in the text either.
- **Effect size:** Numeric means and confidence intervals are in scatter plots (figures 5-6) and not in the text I read. I cannot give a percent improvement.
- **Placebo comparison:** The placebo lotion contained the base formula, so a plain moisturizer effect is built into the comparison. The placebo group still got worse, which the authors attribute to winter conditions.
- **Material outcome:** Not established. Subjects completed skin questionnaires, but those results are not reported in the text I read. A TEWL difference at 2 weeks is a measurable change; whether it is something you would feel is unknown.
- **Adverse events:** No adverse events are described in the text I read. Absence of reporting is not evidence of safety.
- **Funding and conflicts:** Authors declare no conflict of interest. The MVL tested was the ADEKA commercial raw material. I could not confirm author affiliations from what I read.
- **Limits:** One trial, 59 analyzed people, 2 weeks, dry-skin population, no skin-tone data, no follow-up after stopping, one season. The authors also doubled the concentration relative to cell work because the stratum corneum limits permeability (their statement).
  - Water loss (TEWL): the placebo group worsened significantly over the 2 weeks; the MVL group did not change. The between-group difference in change was significant (p<0.05).
  - Stratum corneum water content (Corneometer): rose within the MVL group (p<0.01) but the difference between groups was not significant.
  - Surface lipid: no change within either group; the between-group change favored MVL (p<0.05). The authors note the reading mixes sebum and skin-barrier lipids, so the source is unclear.
  - Lab side: in a 3D epidermis model, TEWL was reported as '27% compared with control' (the wording is ambiguous; check the paper before quoting a number).

- Do not add it on top of a full routine. If it does not replace a step, skip it.
- The trial used a lotion with 0.1% applied twice daily. Use any product as directed; there is no tested dose beyond that.
- Interactions with retinoids, acids and vitamin C are untested. Nothing I found says it conflicts, but that is absence of data. Introduce it alone for the first week.

## WHAT TO EXPECT, AND WHEN
- **Days 1-3:** Probably nothing visible. Check for stinging or new bumps.
- **Weeks 2-4:** If it works as in the trial, less tightness or flaking during dry weather (my inference; the trial measured instruments, not feelings).
- **Beyond 4 weeks:** No data. Collagen or hyaluronic-acid changes have not been shown in people.

## SAFETY
- **Contraindications and unknowns:** None reported in the paper's text. Not tested in pregnancy, in people with skin disease, or on retinoid-treated skin. Treat a 'no reports' as unknown.
- **Side benefits:** None shown in people. The biofilm result is laboratory-only.

## DOES IT TRANSFER?
Does the mechanism apply to deeper skin tones? The pathway (lipid transport into lamellar granules) is not tone-specific, so I would expect it to transfer. The trial gives no tone data, so that is inference. For this ingredient the efficacy question and the risk question separate: it is not an irritant or exfoliant class, so post-inflammatory pigment risk is not the concern; the vehicle's richness is.

## WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE VERDICT
- A second independent trial, ideally 4-8 weeks
- Data in Fitzpatrick IV-VI skin
- A product that discloses the percentage
- Results on retinoid-treated or oily, congestion-prone skin
