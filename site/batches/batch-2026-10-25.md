# [ENTRY] Peptide serum (tripeptide/hexapeptide) after fractional CO2 laser and RF microneedling: two split-face trials
- **Verdict:** WATCH | **Lane:** Clinic and aftercare | **Run:** 2026-10-25
- **Evidence:** 2 of 5. Two small evaluator-blind split-face RCTs (14 days each, exploratory); manufacturer-co-authored. Full text (open access) read
- **Human trial:** yes | **Skin-tone data:** Fitzpatrick II-IV only (Chinese participants); V-VI not included
- **Tags:** aftercare, post-procedure, fractional CO2, radiofrequency microneedling, peptides, edema, TEWL, manufacturer co-authors, Fitzpatrick II-IV, ask the clinician, Canada: cosmetic

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
In two small Chinese split-face trials (51 people, Fitzpatrick II-IV) a peptide serum beat a basic moisturizer on a few recovery measures after laser and RF microneedling, but missed its primary endpoint, showed no pigment advantage, and has manufacturer co-authors.
- Design (full text read): two prospective, randomized, evaluator-blind split-face trials at one centre in China. Fractional CO2 laser (n=26) and RF microneedling (n=25). Serum on one half of the face, a basic moisturizer on the other, for 3 days of intensive use then until day 14; the FCL arm also pre-treated for 2 weeks.
- The primary endpoint (investigator-graded erythema) improved on both sides in both trials, but the difference between sides was not significant.
- After multiplicity correction the serum side did better on: water loss (TEWL) at day 3, and edema, redness and roughness at day 14 in the laser trial; edema at day 7 in the microneedling trial. Subjects (who could not be blinded) preferred the serum side: 81.8% and 100% at day 14.
- Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (graded in the laser trial) improved on both sides by day 14 with no difference between them, so there is no evidence here of a pigment benefit.

## FIT AND REPLACEMENT LOGIC (my judgment, not a measurement)
- **Fit for you:** Partial Partial. Relevant to aftercare for device treatments, which is where thermal injury and pigment risk concentrate. But phototypes V-VI were not included, post-inflammatory marks improved on both sides with no advantage for the serum, and the serum is expensive.
- **Barrier:** Partial. TEWL recovered faster at day 3 on the serum side in the laser trial only; microneedling showed no between-side difference.
- **Retinoid:** Unknown. The trials required a washout of topical retinoids (at least 2 weeks) before enrolling, so use alongside retinoids is untested. Procedure timing relative to retinoids is a question for the treating clinician.
- **Breakout risk:** Unknown. The serum is a water-free, silicone-based formula with soybean oil; breakout risk was not assessed.
- **Season:** Unknown. Not assessed.
- **What it displaces:** One aftercare moisturizer or serum step for the first days after a device treatment. Not a daily-routine step.

## WHAT IT IS
A water-free facial serum from Alastin (Regenerating Skin Nectar) whose headline ingredient is TriHex Technology: the signal peptides palmitoyl tripeptide-1 and palmitoyl hexapeptide-12. It is sold as a pre- and post-procedure product. Percentages are proprietary.

## MECHANISM
Laser or RF microneedling injury [applied] -> Peptides signal matrix remodelling [stated] -> Less edema and redness, faster water-loss recovery on serum side [human] -> Less post-inflammatory pigment [inferred]
1. Thermal devices (fractional CO2 laser, RF microneedling) create controlled injury, followed by redness, swelling, crusting and a leaky barrier.
2. The brand's proposed mechanism: palmitoyl tripeptide-1 (GHK-type) promotes clearance of damaged matrix and collagen synthesis; palmitoyl hexapeptide-12 promotes tropoelastin. The authors call it 'remove, rebuild, replenish'.
3. The trials measured clinical recovery (grades, redness, water loss), not matrix remodelling directly.
- **Inference, not observation:** The matrix-remodelling mechanism is the authors' framing from earlier work; these two trials did not measure collagen or elastin. Any recovery benefit could also come from the vehicle (silicone base, botanicals, arnica) or from simply having an extra, more occlusive product than the comparison lotion.

## STUDIES
### Liu Z, Yi Z, Wang P, Wan L, Ye L, Chen Y, et al. Efficacy and safety of a topical skincare regimen with tripeptide/hexapeptide following aesthetic procedures: analysis of two randomized, split-face trials in Asian subjects. Dermatol Ther (Heidelb). 2026;16(8):4041-4055.
- **Read depth:** Full text (open access) read
- **Design:** Two prospective, randomized, evaluator-blind, split-face trials, single centre in China, registered (ChiCTR2400089516, ChiCTR2400089424). FCL study n=26 (18 women, 8 men): serum on one hemi-face for 2 weeks before, then 3 days intensive, then until day 14. RFMN study n=25 (24 women, 1 man): serum immediately after, 3 days intensive, then until day 14. Comparator: Cetaphil Daily Hydrating Lotion. One blinded dermatologist graded; VISIA redness and AquaFlux water loss measured. Primary endpoint: investigator-assessed erythema (day 7 laser, day 3 RFMN).
- **Who was included:** Adults 18-70, Fitzpatrick II-IV, planning aesthetic procedures; washouts required for topical retinoids (2 weeks), systemic retinoids (6 months) and peels or device treatments (3-6 months). Participants were not blinded because products differ in texture and packaging.
- **Effect size:** Paired effect sizes of about 0.4 to 0.9 (as reported) for the significant secondary endpoints; the primary endpoint's effect is not significant. Raw means are in figures.
- **Placebo comparison:** No vehicle serum. The comparator was a basic hydrating lotion, so the trial cannot say whether the peptides, the silicone-based water-free vehicle or the botanicals drove any difference.
- **Material outcome:** Not established. Graded 0-4 and 0-9 scales were not psychometrically validated (authors' statement); the primary endpoint was not met.
- **Adverse events:** FCL study: 6 mild-to-moderate adverse events in 4 participants, judged mostly unrelated. RFMN study: one mild adverse event.
- **Funding and conflicts:** Two co-authors list Galderma R&D and Galderma Medical Affairs affiliations. The tested product is a branded Alastin serum; I did not verify the corporate relationship between the two. Funding statement wording not extracted.
- **Limits:** Exploratory, no sample-size calculation, 14 days, split-face contamination risk, subjects not blinded, unvalidated scales, two different protocols not directly comparable, Fitzpatrick II-IV only.
  - Primary endpoint: erythema improved within both sides in both trials; between-side differences not significant.
  - Laser trial, after Bonferroni correction: TEWL day 3 (adjusted p=0.011), edema day 14 (adjusted p=0.025), VISIA redness day 14 (adjusted p=0.001), roughness day 14 (p=0.009). Crusting day 3 did not survive correction.

  - PIH (laser trial): improved on both sides by day 14; no between-side difference.
  - Subject preference at day 14 (unblinded): 81.8% (laser) and 100% (microneedling) reported better recovery on the serum side.

# [ENTRY] Topical PDRN (salmon-DNA polymer) versus retinol: a periocular trial with cell and ex vivo data
- **Verdict:** WATCH | **Lane:** Ingredient | **Run:** 2026-10-25
- **Evidence:** 2 of 5. Small split-face RCT (active comparator, 4 weeks) + cell, ex vivo and single-volunteer penetration work. Full text (open access) read
- **Human trial:** yes | **Skin-tone data:** Not reported. Trial population was Chinese women; phototype was not stated in the text I read.
- **Tags:** PDRN, polynucleotide, photoaging, periocular, retinol comparator, sensitive skin, company-linked authors, cosmetic, no vehicle arm, buy in Seoul, ship from EU

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
A topical PDRN eye cream beat an identical 0.1% retinol cream on wrinkle and dermal-thickness measures over 4 weeks in 31 sensitive-skinned Chinese women, but the authors are company-linked, there was no vehicle-only arm, and nothing here speaks to deep skin or pigment.
- Design (full text read): randomized, double-blind, split-face, 31 healthy Chinese women aged 35-55 with self-reported sensitive skin. One eye area got a water-in-silicone cream with 0.1% PDRN-850K (salmon-derived DNA, average molecular weight up to 850 kDa); the other got an identical cream with 0.1% retinol. Twice daily for 28 days, daily sunscreen required.
- Reported result: at day 28 crow's-feet wrinkle area and number fell by about 20-23% on the PDRN side versus about 6-7% on the retinol side. Ultrasound dermal thickness and density, tear-trough and eye-bag measures, and elasticity and firmness were reported as roughly 1.8 to 2 times greater. Exact values are in figures I could not read numerically.
- What weakens it: there was no vehicle-only arm, so the study cannot show PDRN beats a plain cream; 4 weeks is early for retinol; the authors themselves say early ultrasound changes may partly reflect plumping or hydration. Authors are affiliated with companies (a skin-aging research institute and a biotech firm) and the PDRN was supplied by a manufacturer; I did not see a conflict-of-interest statement in the text I read.
- The penetration evidence is indirect: Raman signal in a lab-grown epidermis, a porcine skin experiment at 0.1% in solution, and one human volunteer whose forearm showed a relative signal of about 3% at 24 hours. The authors state Raman cannot separate added PDRN from the skin's own nucleic acids.

## FIT AND REPLACEMENT LOGIC (my judgment, not a measurement)
- **Fit for you:** Partial Partial. The cell and ex vivo work is mechanism-level and not skin-tone specific as far as I know, but the one clinical trial was 31 Chinese women (phototype not reported), measured wrinkles and dermal thickness rather than pigment, and compared against retinol, not a prescription retinoid.
- **Barrier:** Partial. Tolerated by a sensitive-skin cohort with no adverse reactions reported, but the trial did not measure barrier function (no water-loss readings).
- **Retinoid:** Unknown. Tested against retinol, never alongside it, so I cannot say whether it adds to a retinoid or lets you cut one back.
- **Breakout risk:** Unknown. Not assessed; the tested cream was an eye cream in a water-in-silicone base.
- **Season:** Unknown. Trial dates were late September to October 2025; no seasonal analysis.
- **What it displaces:** A PDRN step if the routine already has one (a swap, not an addition). It does not replace a prescription retinoid: the comparator was over-the-counter retinol.

## WHAT IT IS
PDRN (polydeoxyribonucleotide) is a mix of DNA fragments, here a salmon-derived preparation of 96% purity or more with an average molecular weight up to 850 kDa (about 1,200 base pairs), called PDRN-850K by the authors. It is best known as an injectable skin booster. This paper tests it as a cream.

## MECHANISM
0.1% PDRN-850K eye cream, twice daily [applied] -> PDRN-associated signal in viable epidermis [stated] -> PI3K-Akt, TGF-beta/Smad and autophagy signalling up [cell] -> More collagen and elastic-fibre proteins in UV-damaged skin [cell] -> Wrinkle area, dermal thickness and elasticity better than retinol at day 28 [human] -> Lasting dermal remodelling [inferred]
1. In cultured human dermal fibroblasts, PDRN-850K raised phosphorylation of PI3K and AKT, TGF-beta and SMAD2/3, and autophagy markers (LC3-II up, p62 down). Blocking each pathway with an inhibitor reduced the rise in collagen and elastin gene expression (n=3 experiments).
2. Delivery: Raman signal rose over 2, 6 and 24 hours in a lab-grown epidermis and fell with an endocytosis inhibitor; fluorescent-labelled PDRN at 0.1% in solution reached the dermis of pig skin by 8-12 hours; in one volunteer, the eye cream showed signal beyond 20 micrometres at 8-24 hours.
3. In UV-damaged human ex vivo skin (n=3), the 0.1% cream increased viable epidermal thickness and staining for collagens I, III, IV, V, VI, VII, XV, XVII, XVIII, elastin, fibrillin-1 and YAP.
4. In the split-face trial, wrinkle, dermal-thickness and elasticity measures changed more on the PDRN side than the retinol side.
- **Inference, not observation:** The pathway chain (PI3K-Akt, TGF-beta/Smad, autophagy to matrix genes) comes from cells, and pathway activation was tested in unstressed fibroblasts, not UV-damaged ones. That the cream delivers enough intact PDRN into dermis in living human skin is not shown. The authors call their own penetration data supportive, not conclusive.

## STUDIES
### Ye R, Wang Q, Du L, Li L, Hu F. Topical medium-length PDRN enhances dermal extracellular matrix repair in photodamaged skin via PI3K-Akt/TGF-beta-regulated pathways. PLoS One. 2026;21(7):e0350905.
- **Read depth:** Full text (open access) read
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, split-face, self-controlled. 31 healthy women aged 35-55 (mean 47.5), self-reported sensitive skin (Huaxi questionnaire 18-33), periocular lines and eye bags. 0.1% PDRN-850K eye cream versus identical-base 0.1% retinol eye cream, twice daily for 28 days with daily sunscreen. Visits at baseline, day 14 and day 28. Instruments: VISIA 7, Antera 3D, Cutometer, ultrasound. Single site in China; ethics approval September 2025.
- **Who was included:** Healthy Chinese women. Excluded: pregnancy or lactation, systemic or severe skin disease, known allergy to retinol or PDRN, and recent periocular retinoids or nucleic-acid products. Skin tone or Fitzpatrick type is not reported in the text I read.
- **Effect size:** Statistical results are shown in figure 6 as means with standard errors; the text gives only relative differences (about 1.8 to 2 times) and the wrinkle percentages above. I could not extract p-values or confidence intervals from the text.
- **Placebo comparison:** No vehicle-only arm. The comparator is 0.1% retinol, so the study shows PDRN did better than retinol under these conditions, not that PDRN beats a plain moisturiser. Retinol at 0.1% for 4 weeks is also an early readout for retinol.
- **Material outcome:** Not established. The instrument differences are measured changes (wrinkle area, ultrasound thickness); no patient-rated outcome or blinded clinician grade is reported in the text I read. The authors warn early ultrasound changes may reflect hydration or plumping.
- **Adverse events:** None reported in the text I read.
- **Funding and conflicts:** Authors list affiliations with a skin-aging research institute and a biotechnology company; the PDRN-850K was provided by Ruijiming (Shandong) Biotechnology Co., Ltd., and retinol by DSM-Firmenich. I did not find a funding or competing-interests statement in the text I read; check the paper's front matter.
- **Limits:** One trial, 31 people, 4 weeks, one site, eye area only, women only, sensitive-skin cohort. Cell work in 3 experiments. Raman cannot distinguish added from endogenous nucleic acids. In vivo penetration is a single person. Pathway activation was not tested under UV stress. No deep-skin or pigment data.
  - Wrinkles (VISIA image analysis): crow's-feet area and number down about 20-23% at day 28 on the PDRN side versus about 6-7% on the retinol side; similar pattern for infraorbital wrinkles. Benefit appeared by day 14 on the PDRN side.
  - Dermal structure (ultrasound): dermal thickness and density increased about two-fold more on the PDRN side (authors' description; values in figure 6).
  - Eye bags and tear trough (Antera 3D): reductions about two-fold greater on the PDRN side.
  - Elasticity (R2) and firmness (F4): improvements about 1.8-fold higher on the PDRN side.
  - Tolerability: both creams tolerated; no adverse reactions reported.

## CLAIMS CHECK
